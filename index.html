Pizza Delivery Night

Week 4 of the Mites half-ice series. Wednesday, October 7, 2026, 5:10–6:00 PM.

Week 3 taught the pass. Tonight we teach the catch, and every stop is a pizza stop. The puck is a pizza: deliver it flat, catch it soft, and don't spill the toppings.

  • Passing: catching the pass
  • Skating: stops, turns, scooter pushes
  • Fun: every block is a game
“Pizza!”

Tonight's freeze call. Yell it and every skater does a pizza stop, puts their stick on the ice, and looks at you. Use it every time you need their attention. Each one is a free stopping rep.

Tonight's numbers

Change these at the rink and the whole plan updates: block times, groups, coach jobs, diagrams, gear counts, and the bench card. Defaults are your usual Wednesday slot with 12 skaters and 3 coaches.

Run of show

    The blocks

    1

    Chef Says

    Warm-up game

    Legs moving the second skates hit the ice. Every order on the menu is a skating skill (stopping, gliding, balancing, getting up), so they practice all of it inside a game they already know.

    Set up

    Whole zone, no cones. Pucks in a bucket at the blue line for the second half.

    Run it

    1. Everyone skates around the zone. The head coach is the chef and calls orders from the menu.
    2. Only follow orders that start with “Chef says.” Get tricked? Do a pepperoni drop and keep playing. Nobody sits out.
    3. Halfway through: “Chef says grab a pizza!” Dump the pucks and keep calling orders with a puck on every stick.

    The menu

    Pizza!
    Snowplow stop. Heels out, make snow.
    French fries!
    Glide on two feet, skates straight, knees bent.
    Stretchy cheese!
    Glide on one foot, other leg stretched back.
    Pepperoni drop!
    Both knees down, pop right back up.
    Spin the dough!
    Turn all the way around.
    Hot oven!
    Five fast strides.
    Order up!
    Freeze in ready position: knees bent, stick on the ice.

    Say this

    • “Knees bent!”
    • “Make snow!”
    • “Pop up!”

    Level up

    Let a confident skater be chef for two orders. Chain orders together: “Chef says pizza… then hot oven!”

    Make it easier

    Drop the trick and just call orders. Skip the puck half if it's getting chaotic.

    Watch for

    Leaning back on stops, so they fall backward: “Chest up, knees bent, push both heels out.” Kids bunching around the chef: keep moving so the group spreads out.

    Coach jobs

      2

      Delivery Route

      Skating

      All of tonight's skating in one loop: big pushes, pizza stops, leaning into turns, and scooter pushes, which are the first step toward crossovers and the next step up from Week 3's circle edge work. Two loops keep the lines short.

      Set up

      Two mirror-image loops, one on each side of the zone (6 skaters each). Each loop gets a start cone on the blue line, 3 slalom cones about 6 feet apart, a doorstep cone just outside the faceoff circle, the circle itself as the roundabout, and a coach holding a stick across the path as the tunnel.

      Run it

      1. Release one skater every 3 seconds. If more than two are waiting at the start, release faster.
      2. Twisty road: weave the slalom.
      3. Doorstep: pizza stop at the cone. “Ding-dong, pizza!”
      4. Roundabout: around the outside of the faceoff circle with scooter pushes. Glide on the inside foot, push with the outside foot.
      5. Tunnel: duck under the coach's stick and sit low like a dough ball.
      6. Hot oven: race to the blue line, pizza stop, glide back to the start, and go again.
      7. Halfway: the groups switch loops so everyone turns both directions.

      Say this

      • “Big pushes!”
      • “Lean like a bike!”
      • “Scooter push!”

      Level up

      Carry a puck the whole route: deliver the pizza. On the roundabout, try stepping the outside foot over the inside one (a crossover).

      Make it easier

      Two-foot glide around the circle instead of scooter pushes. Swap the tunnel for a stick laid flat on the ice to step over.

      Watch for

      Running on tiptoes instead of gliding: “Push out to the side, then glide.” Standing tall on the circle: “Inside shoulder down, knees bent.” Tunnel safety: hold the stick loosely at your smallest skater's shoulder height so it gives if someone bumps it.

      Coach jobs

        3

        Catch the Pizza

        Passing

        Most mite passes don't fail at the pass. They fail at the catch: the receiver's stick is up in the air or stiff as a board. Tonight every pass needs a target, and every catch has to stop on the blade.

        Set up

        Partners face each other three stick lengths apart, with every pair passing the same direction so loose pucks don't cross: 6 pairs. Do a 10-second demo with another coach, then go.

        The catch, in three words

        Landing pad
        Blade flat on the ice, facing your partner.
        Tap-tap
        Tap your stick twice to say “I'm ready.”
        Soft hands
        Let the blade give a little as the puck arrives, like catching an egg.

        The pass, from Week 3: sweep it with no wind-up, and finish with your blade pointing at your partner's landing pad.

        Run it: three rounds, new partner each round

        1. Counter pickup (standing still). The pizza has to stop on your blade before you send it back. Count clean catches to 10.
        2. Bigger delivery zone. After every 3 clean catches, the passer takes one big step back. Pairs climb at their own pace.
        3. Drive-by. The receiver glides slowly across in front of the passer, and the passer aims just in front of them. Switch jobs after 4 passes.

        Partner switch: the blue-line side slides one spot down the line, and the skater on the end skates around to the other end. Team challenge: after each round every pair shouts its catch count, you add it up, and the next round tries to beat it.

        Say this

        • “Tap-tap!”
        • “Landing pad!”
        • “Soft hands!”

        Level up

        Backhand catches. One-touch passes, with no stopping, for pairs who are perfect.

        Make it easier

        Two stick lengths apart. Do Round 1 on one knee so they can focus on their hands.

        Watch for

        Stick in the air as the pass comes: “Landing pad, glue it down.” Puck bouncing off the blade: “Soft hands, catch the egg.” Puck sliding under the blade means the heel is lifted, often from a stick that's too long (see the coach's notes). Big wind-ups: “Puck starts on your blade, then sweep.”

        Coach jobs

          Water and reset. Bottles go on the boards at the blue line before practice so this takes two minutes, not five. While kids drink, coaches turn the route cones into bowling pins and line pucks up on the blue line.

            4

            Pizza Bowling

            Passing game

            A pass that knocks a cone over is instant, loud feedback. Kids take dozens of aimed passes without it ever feeling like a drill, and they skate hard to chase every puck.

            Set up

            3 lanes running in from the blue line. Three tall cones per lane in a triangle are the pins, with the head pin about four stick lengths in. Skaters line up side by side on the blue line, each with a puck.

            Run it

            1. Rapid fire: one skater at a time down the line takes a sweep pass at the pins, chases the puck, and stickhandles back around the outside of the lane to the end of the line.
            2. Strike (all three down): the whole lane yells “Pizza strike!” and the skater who knocked down the last pin sets them back up.
            3. Round 2: pins move back one stick length. Keep going.

            Say this

            • “Eyes on the pins!”
            • “Sweep it!”
            • “Point at the head pin!”

            Level up

            Backhand passes only. Lanes next to the boards can try banking it off the boards.

            Make it easier

            Move the pins closer, or add two cones to make the target wider. If your cones are too heavy to tip, any hit counts.

            Watch for

            Slap shots that lift the puck: “It's a pass, not a shot. Keep it on the ice.” Anyone waiting more than about 10 seconds: split that lane in two.

            Coach jobs

              5

              Two-Topping Hockey

              Small-area games

              Games are where passing becomes real. The two-point rule rewards a pass without forcing one, so the game keeps flowing and passing still wins.

              Set up

              Mini nets go against the side boards, facing each other. One team in each game wears pinnies; the other plays in jerseys. Slide the big net back against the end boards.

              Rules

              1. Goal = 1 point (plain cheese). A goal right after a pass between teammates = 2 points (two toppings).
              2. Coach on the wall: a coach stands at the edge of each game as a neutral passer. Pass to the coach and get it back. It counts as a pass.
              3. Puck out of play, or a goal? The coach rolls a new puck in right away: “Fresh pizza!” No faceoffs.
              5. Halfway: 30-second water stop, then switch which net each team attacks. Move a player or two if a game is lopsided.

              Say this

              • “Tap-tap!”
              • “Heads up!”
              • “Two toppings!”

              Level up

              Three toppings: two passes before a goal is worth 3.

              Make it easier

              Stop keeping score and just cheer every pass.

              Watch for

              Everyone chasing the puck in one bunch, the beehive. That's normal at this age, so praise the kid who stays open: “Great spot, you're open for the pizza!” Sticks up high: “Sticks on the ice.”

              Coach jobs

                6

                Pizza Party Shootout

                Finisher and huddle

                Ends the night with tonight's skill, catching a pass on the move, in the moment they love most: scoring.

                Set up

                Reuse Game A's nets right where they are, so there's nothing to move.

                Run it

                1. The skater heads toward the net, taps twice, catches the coach's pass, and shoots.
                2. The next skater goes as soon as the shot is taken. Keep it moving; the line cheers every goal.
                3. Second time through is skater's choice: start with your own puck and show your best move.
                4. Huddle for the last 2 minutes, on one knee with sticks down. “Show me your landing pad!” and every blade goes flat. Name the Delivery Driver of the Day for a great catch, pass, or teammate moment, and they lead the cheer: “1, 2, 3… PIZZA!”

                For parents on the way out: “Ask your skater to show you tap-tap and a pizza stop.”

                Say this

                • “Tap-tap!”
                • “Catch it, then shoot!”
                • “Pizza delivered!”

                Level up

                Shoot it without stopping it first: a one-timer.

                Make it easier

                The coach slides the puck right onto the stick, and the skater shoots from a standstill.

                Watch for

                Lines longer than 5 or 6: open another target or let the waiting kids stickhandle in place. A kid who keeps missing gets a bonus delivery so everyone leaves with a goal.

                Coach jobs

                  Coach's notes for tonight

                  • Cheer the catch

                    Praise receivers by name as loudly as scorers: “Great landing pad, Sam!” Kids repeat whatever gets cheered.

                  • One kid, one fix

                    Pick one thing per kid per block. Three corrections at once means none of them stick.

                  • Ten-second demos

                    Show it, say one cue, go. If they don't get it, fix it while they're moving, not in a huddle.

                  • Pair by ability, mix for games

                    Similar-level partners for Catch the Pizza so both get success. Mixed teams for Two-Topping Hockey so the games stay close.

                  • Check stick length

                    On skates, the top of the stick should land around the chin. Longer than that lifts the heel of the blade and pucks slide right under it, a common hidden reason a kid “can't catch.” Mention it to parents after practice.

                  • Falls are on the menu

                    Call every fall a pepperoni drop and cheer the pop-up. Falling stays normal and nobody gets embarrassed.

                  • Water is set-up time

                    Coaches move cones while kids drink. Kids should never stand around waiting for adults.

                  • Pucks have a home

                    One bucket at the blue line is the pizza box. In every transition, extra pucks go in the box.

                  Gear list

                  Counts update with tonight's numbers. Tap to check items off as you pack.
